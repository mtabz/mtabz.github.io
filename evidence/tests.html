Engineering judgement

Test strategy

The suite is organised around the product decisions that must remain trustworthy as registry data, adapters and the vendor corpus evolve.

Deterministic rulesAdversarial scenariosReal registry shapes

Four layers of confidence

01

Resolution rules

Checks that bare-name, scoped, differently named and remote official surfaces resolve to the same result every time.

Why it matters: the product’s core claim is reproducibility, so name-shape edge cases cannot be left to model judgement.

02

Adversarial scenarios

Exercises third-party occupiers, community forks, no-provenance decoys, unclaimed names and vendor-owned controls.

Why it matters: the resolver must distinguish urgent exposure, latent risk and a clean result rather than treating every difference as suspicious.

03

Registry contracts

Replays frozen, redacted responses from npm, PyPI and the official MCP Registry without making the tests depend on a live network.

Why it matters: adapters are tested against realistic API shapes while runs remain stable and repeatable.

04

Output consistency

Checks that the Markdown audit and JSON exposure map describe the same surface state, provenance and finding.

Why it matters: human and machine consumers must act on one source of truth.

Scenario design

The corpus combines representative real vendor shapes with planted adversarial cases. Real examples expose naming and registry edge cases; planted fixtures make the expected answer unambiguous, including a deliberate no-provenance decoy and clean controls.

Why not just end-to-end tests?

A single passing scan would not reveal whether a failure came from naming rules, a registry adapter, provenance logic or report rendering. The layers keep those responsibilities separable.

What changes safely

  • Add new registry adapters
  • Expand the vendor corpus
  • Refine provenance rules
  • Change the UI without changing classifications
